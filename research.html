Research foundation

Problem, gap, objectives and methodology

The project responds to limitations in coverage, connectivity and response time by combining multiple field signals with edge-based AI processing.

Introduction

Landslide risk in Sri Lanka

Landslides are among the common natural hazards affecting Sri Lanka, especially in hilly areas with high rainfall such as Badulla, Nuwara Eliya and Kegalle. Heavy rainfall, soil saturation and slope instability are highlighted in the project presentation as major contributors.

Because landslides can lead to loss of life, property damage and environmental destruction, continuous monitoring and faster warning delivery are central to the research motivation.

Research problem

How can an early detection system help reduce the impact of landslides?

The presentation identifies four practical limitations in current monitoring approaches.

1

Severe consequences

Landslides can cause deaths, property damage and infrastructure destruction.

2

Coverage gaps

Existing monitoring systems are not available in every vulnerable area.

3

Weak connectivity

Poor mobile or internet coverage can delay warning messages.

4

Manual monitoring limits

Continuous human observation is impractical and unreliable for real-time detection.

Research gap

Current approaches do not fully solve field deployment constraints

  • Some systems focus on only one or two factors rather than multiple contributors such as rainfall, soil and ground movement.
  • Cloud-based processing can become unreliable in areas with poor internet connectivity.
  • Remote processing can introduce warning delays.
  • High costs and complex setups can make rural deployment difficult.

The proposed direction therefore prioritizes local processing, multi-sensor inputs and an affordable, deployable field system.

Research objectives

Eight detailed research objectives

The project combines sensing, automated rainfall drainage, on-device AI, location awareness, remote monitoring and community warnings.

Main objective

Design and develop an IoT-based landslide early detection system that uses Edge AI and automated rainfall monitoring to support reliable, timely landslide risk alerts in Sri Lanka.

01

Multi-sensor data collection

To design a sensor-based monitoring system that collects rainfall, soil moisture, vibration, and ground-tilt data relevant to landslide risk.

02

Automated rainfall monitoring

To develop an automated rainfall monitoring unit capable of measuring rainfall and automatically draining collected water using an electronically controlled solenoid valve.

03

Edge Impulse classification

To develop and deploy a machine-learning model using Edge Impulse for classifying landslide risk based on sensor inputs.

04

ESP32 Edge AI inference

To perform Edge AI inference directly on an ESP32 so that preliminary risk classification can be carried out locally.

05

GPS location association

To integrate GPS information with the monitoring system in order to associate sensor readings and warning events with the monitored location.

06

GSM and Firebase monitoring

To use GSM communication and Firebase Realtime Database for storing and remotely viewing live and historical monitoring data.

07

LoRa community warning

To implement LoRa-based long-range communication for transmitting dangerous-condition alerts to a separate community alarm unit.

08

Integrated prototype evaluation

To evaluate the functionality of the integrated prototype by testing sensor acquisition, AI prediction, rainfall communication, GPS, LoRa warning transmission, automated drainage, and Firebase data uploading.

System methodology

Sense → process locally → communicate → warn

Soil moisture, tilt/ground movement, rainfall and GPS data are collected by field sensors and sent to the main controller. Edge AI performs local prediction, while communication through LoRa / GSM / Wi-Fi supports monitoring and alert delivery.

The system architecture also includes solar power and battery support for field operation.

AI workflow

Model development with Edge Impulse

The presentation shows a workflow that moves from dataset preparation and data cleaning into feature extraction/engineering, model training, model testing and final deployment.

Random Forest and k-Nearest Neighbors were both implemented and tested; the Random Forest model was selected as the final model.

Landslide hazard context

Why rainfall and ground movement must be monitored together

Heavy or prolonged rainfall can increase water infiltration and soil saturation, contributing to slope instability. Soil moisture, vibration and ground tilt provide complementary indicators of changing ground conditions. These measurements help the prototype classify risk but do not guarantee that a landslide will be detected or predicted.

Sri Lankan highland locations, including Nuwara Eliya, Badulla and Kegalle, are relevant to the hazard context. The National Building Research Organisation (NBRO) provides landslide hazard zonation work; site-specific risk decisions should use official guidance alongside field data.

Relevant background research includes rainfall-triggered landslides in Nuwara Eliya [6], landslide hazard potential in Hanguranketha [5] and NBRO hazard mapping [10].
